iPhone · Life simulation

Every hour matters. A whole life underneath.

You start at 18 with almost nothing and a town you can walk around. There is no menu of abstract buttons — if you want a job you walk to the office and apply at the hiring board, and if you want money in the bank you walk to the bank and speak to a teller.

What the game actually is

Every hour is spent

A shift, a class, a workout, a night's sleep — each costs roughly what it would cost in life. Study the afternoon away and you did not earn that afternoon.

Monday settles everything

Wages land, rent leaves, tuition bills, interest compounds, and legal trouble moves one step forward — all as a consequence of a week you already chose.

There is always a shortcut

And it always costs something. The game does not lecture you about it. It just runs the consequences, across a whole life.

A town that holds it all

Seventeen destinations with their own counters and staff, traffic on its own schedule, and people walking their own routes.

What it isn't

No energy timers. No waiting for a bar to refill. No pay-to-win — nothing purchasable makes you stronger, and no amount of real money buys a better life in the game. The only purchase is one that removes ads.

Mature systems — crime, substances, dependence, and their consequences — can be switched off entirely at character creation. Play begins at 18.
